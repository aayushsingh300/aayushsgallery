01 Titan Eyeplus · E-commerce

Make online eyewear feel certain.

Redesigning the journey from “will these suit me?” to a confident prescription purchase—for one of India’s largest eyewear retailers.

UX designWeb + mobile web2021

01 / Premise

The stores already felt expert. The website made customers feel like they were shopping alone.

Titan Eyeplus had retail scale, trained optometrists and years of brand trust. Online, that advantage disappeared behind crowded promotions, brittle mobile flows and a purchase model that treated prescription eyewear like any other product.

02 / The disconnect

A premium service at the store.
A bargain bin on the screen.

The design problem began before usability. Customers who knew the physical stores could not recognise the same care or authority online.

The inherited experience

Everything was present.
Nothing was in charge.

Products, categories, promotions and virtual try-on competed at the same level. On mobile, the desktop catalogue compressed into an even noisier version of itself.

03 / Reframing the brief

Conversion was the metric.
Uncertainty was the problem.

01 / Suitability

“Will it suit my face?”

Style, scale and fit are hard to judge from a flat product image. Without confidence, browsing becomes endless comparison.

Design response · recommendation in context
02 / Accuracy

“Will they get my prescription right?”

A medical detail sits inside a retail transaction. The flow needed the clarity of a form and the reassurance of an optometrist.

Design response · staged lens decisions
03 / Price

“What will I actually pay?”

Coupons, loyalty points and lens costs appeared across separate steps. The total only became legible at the moment of commitment.

Design response · one visible bill
Design thesis

Do not reproduce the store on a screen. Reproduce the store’s ability to make the next decision feel safe.

04 / Research lens

Two shopping postures.
One architecture.

Research clustered around two recurring behaviours, not demographic personas. One shopper needed careful guidance; the other wanted the site to remember what they already knew.

Guide meThe hesitant first-timer

Needs fit explanation, prescription reassurance and visible returns before committing.

Get me thereThe returning buyer

Needs remembered preferences, efficient filters and a short route back to a trusted frame.

06 / Orient

Make the homepage
a useful front door.

We moved from a wall of campaigns to a category-first browsing model. Marketing still had room to move, but the customer’s route into the catalogue no longer depended on reading a banner.

  • Intent-led categories before offers
  • Retail-scale proof near the first decision
  • Store discovery remains part of the journey

07 / Narrow

Let people browse widely.
Then make it personal.

A catalogue this large needs structure without making shoppers define themselves too early. Persistent filters support deliberate search; “My Fit” offers a softer layer for people who know the feeling they want, not the terminology.

Feature move / My Fit

Recommendation without a detour.

Instead of sending shoppers into a separate quiz or camera flow, the recommendation layer lives directly inside the catalogue. Turn it on, keep browsing, and preserve every familiar control.

Old pattern

Leave the catalogue → complete a flow → start over

Shipped pattern

Express intent → narrow the same catalogue → stay oriented

08 / Judge

Answer the questions
the product image cannot.

The product page was reorganised around fit confidence: what the frame looks like, how large it is, whether it supports the customer’s lenses, when it will arrive and what happens if it is wrong.

01

Dimensions over labels

Bridge, temple and lens width replace the ambiguity of “medium.”

02

Care before fashion

Delivery and expert help stay adjacent to the buying decision.

03

Action within reach

The primary purchase control persists on narrow screens without hiding the product context.

09 / Commit

One cart.
One honest total.

The old journey distributed discounts across three separate dialogs. We brought coupons, Encircle points, Tata Neu rewards and the itemised bill into one visible decision surface.

11 / Reflection

What the project changed
in our practice.

Eyewear showed us that e-commerce is not always a funnel problem. When the product is personal, medical and aesthetic at once, progress comes from resolving uncertainty in the right order.

The most durable design move was not a new feature. It was deciding what each screen needed to make true before asking the customer to continue.

Working principleConfidence first. Commitment second.
