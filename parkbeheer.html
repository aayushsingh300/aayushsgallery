Parkbeheer NL · Enterprise product design · 2018

Forty printouts.
One operational picture.

We turned a patchwork of spreadsheets, email and legacy terminals into a single place to monitor 923,000 parking spaces across 42 municipalities.

A three-person agency design team delivered this, in weekly feedback loops with the parking officers who would use it. Our remit ran from screen design and interaction patterns through to rollout.

01 / The operating reality

This was not a dashboard problem.
It was a fragmentation problem.

A simple question—“Which car parks are falling behind on EV compliance?”—could require three browser tabs, two spreadsheets and a paper notebook.

The organisation did not lack data. It lacked a shared picture of what was happening. Officers spent their time reconciling sources instead of acting on the result.

“We print forty pages of spreadsheets every Monday morning.”

06 / The product, not the promise

Three views replaced
three kinds of uncertainty.

01 / Locate

See the network as a place.

The map was not in the original brief. Field rides made the gap obvious: officers understood capacity through neighbourhoods and zones, while the old tools only exposed rows.

  • Clustered sites at national scale
  • Zone and status filters remain in context
  • Preview a site before committing to detail

02 / Inspect

One record, every consequence.

A car park is more than a count. Capacity, operator, allocation, EV provision, documents and status history had to read as one coherent record.

  • Stable summary before editable detail
  • Related dimensions grouped, not scattered
  • History stays attached to the decision

03 / Act

Filter once. Act on the set.

Bulk export was not in phase one. In testing, four of five officers reached for it immediately. We brought selection, export and batch updates into the table itself.

  • Persistent filter chips show the active query
  • Bulk actions appear only after selection
  • CSV export preserves reporting workflows

07 / Core workflow · Bulk change

Make the fast path
the safe path.

Bulk actions solved the loudest efficiency problem—and introduced the project’s highest-risk interaction. A single careless edit could change dozens of municipal records. Speed without review was not a solution.

48 selected records×1 shared change=48 chances to be wrong
parkbeheer.nl / car-parks / bulk-reviewGUARDED ACTION
01

Explicit field scope

Only checked fields change. Untouched values are visibly preserved, removing ambiguity from partial updates.

02

Record-level preview

Representative outcomes translate an abstract batch into concrete before-and-after states.

03

Conflict protection

Recently edited or archived records are excluded—not silently overwritten by stale selection state.

04

Permission routing

Changes outside an officer’s authority continue to approval while valid records remain ready.

08 / Beyond the happy path

The interface was only half
of the system.

Government software has a second user: the organisation itself.

Rules change. Officers join and leave. Managers need evidence. Development teams need consistent chart behaviour. We designed the operating layer around the screens so the product could remain trustworthy after handoff.

What we would do differently now

Use real data in round one. Dummy car-park numbers made early feedback abstract. The moment we used officers’ own records, the sessions became specific and useful.

Argue for spatial context earlier. The map became one of the strongest views, but we waited too long to push it into scope.

Escalate uncertainty sooner. Early on we sometimes polished a weak direction before putting the question on the table. The better habit is to expose the question early.

Good enterprise software does not make complexity disappear.

It gives everyone the same, trustworthy picture of it.
