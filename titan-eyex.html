01 / Premise

The brief sounded like an app. The real design problem lived across three surfaces: the glasses, the phone and the world around the wearer.

EyeX used open-ear audio so people could stay connected without losing awareness of their surroundings. That made the experience safer and more social—but also created a new mental model. People understood “glasses” and “earphones.” They did not yet understand the space between them.

04 / Research

Listen for the expectation gap.

Interviews spanned spectacle wearers, non-wearers and people already comfortable with smart accessories. We tested value before polishing screens.

User interviewsCompetitive analysisPrototype testingStakeholder workshops
01

“Show me whether it’s connected.”

Device and battery status were not utility details; they were the foundation of trust. The two temples could discharge independently, so the home screen had to explain both at a glance.

02

“Don’t make my glasses another screen.”

People wanted calls, music and navigation because they reduced phone use. Secondary tools had to remain voluntary and quiet.

03

Eye care was the brand’s unfair advantage.

Users valued guided assessments and healthy-screen reminders more than our early assumptions predicted. It connected the technology back to Titan Eye+’s reason to exist.

Three groups, one shared baseline

We spoke with spectacle wearers, non-wearers and smart-accessory users across three age bands. Their appetite for bonus features changed; their need for a dependable connection state did not.

06 / The design pivot

When illustration failed,
the product taught the product.

Early onboarding used friendly illustrations. Tests showed that friendliness was not clarity: people still could not tell which temple to touch, which way to swipe or what feedback to expect.

Reframe↘
3D → motion → code

We modelled the glasses in Cinema 4D, rendered them in KeyShot, animated gestures in After Effects, then shipped them as lightweight Lottie sequences. Each instruction showed the exact object, action and response.

02 / Pair & status

Make invisible states feel physical.

Charging, waking, searching and pairing each get a literal visual state. Once connected, the home screen keeps the frame and its two independently draining temple batteries in the highest-priority position.

03 / Eye care

Turn the brand promise into product utility.

Research pulled eye care into the roadmap and extended the project. With Titan’s optometry team, we designed five guided assessments plus contact-lens, blink and 20–20–20 reminders—all voluntary and easy to silence.

DuochromeVisual acuityCentral visionColour blindnessAstigmatism
05 tests

Care without coercion.

Every wellbeing prompt is reversible. The wearer can use individual checks, keep reminders, or silence the system completely.

04 / Fitness

Useful movement, lightly worn.

The motion sensor interprets head movement as activity. We borrowed familiar fitness conventions—goals, rings and day/week/month views—so the unusual input never created an unusual learning curve.

05 / Remote tools

Let the frame do things a phone cannot.

The My Device area turns a temple gesture into a camera shutter or presentation control. Find My Device handles both sides of failure: ring nearby glasses, or show their last location after disconnection.

06 / System depth

The quiet screens make the product complete.

Notifications, profile data and measurement units are not hero moments, but they make fitness, reminders and cross-device use reliable. The system respects local units and keeps every optional behavior editable.

10 / Looking back

The app was not the interface.
The relationship was.

My early instinct was to give exciting interactions—music, fitness, camera control—the loudest voice. Research corrected that. People became curious only after they felt oriented.

The strongest work was not one screen. It was the connective tissue: honest device states, literal gesture teaching, voluntary wellbeing tools and a visual language built from the object itself.

Make the unfamiliar legible.
Then let the magic happen.
